The Civil Suit Journey: From Plaint to Decree

An interactive structural guide to the procedural lifecycle of a civil suit under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. Distinguishes the Ordinary Trial Stream from Optional Interlocutory Relief, Settlement Off-Ramps, Final Adjudication, Appellate/Revisional Recourse, and Execution.

⚖️ Civil Procedural Architecture: Stream Distinctions for Judicial Aspirants

Educational Note: A civil suit does not proceed in an inflexible single-track queue where all 14 stages occur sequentially. The Code establishes a Main Procedural Backbone intersected by Interlocutory Branches, Alternative Dispute Resolution off-ramps, and Conditional Recourse:

1. Ordinary Trial Stream (Stages 1–11):
Inception → Plaint (O. VII) → Process/Summons (O. V) → WS (O. VIII) → First Hearing/Issues (O. XIV) → Evidence on Affidavit (O. XVIII) → Arguments.
2. Optional Interlocutory Relief (Active Across Stages):
Temporary Injunctions (O. XXXIX), Receiver (O. XL), Attachment Before Judgment (O. XXXVIII) — may arise at any time from plaint to decree.
3. Settlement & Alternate Paths (Off-Ramps):
Section 89 ADR (Mediation, Lok Adalat, Arbitration, Conciliation) & Compromise (O. XXIII) — may resolve and terminate the suit without trial.
4. Adjudication & Recourse (Stages 12–14):
Judgment (S. 33, O. XX) → Formal Decree (S. 2(2)) → Execution (O. XXI), with Appeals (Ss. 96, 100), Review (S. 114) and Revision (S. 115) as conditional recourse.

## 1. Executive Summary & Curriculum Classification

In accordance with the official UPPSC UP PCS-J examination syllabus (Advertisement No. A-6/E-1/2026), **nine special social enactments** are incorporated into **Paper I (General Knowledge)** to test a judicial candidate's sensitivity, practical knowledge, and statutory command of welfare, non-discrimination, and protection regimes.

Unlike Paper II procedural and substantive law subjects that emphasize extensive technical pleading or complex evidentiary rules, **Paper I Social Legislation questions test:**
1. **Statutory Definitions & Thresholds:** Objective ages, percentage disability criteria, limitation periods, committee compositions.
2. **Statutory Authorities & Quasi-Judicial Tribunals:** Powers of Maintenance Tribunals, Appropriate Authorities, Internal Committees, Child Welfare Committees, and Special Courts.
3. **Offences, Penalties & Aggravated Thresholds:** Mandatory minimum punishments, repeat offender penalties, compoundability, and non-bailable classifications.
4. **Presumptions of Guilt & Burden Shifting:** Reverse onus clauses under POCSO (Sections 29 & 30), Dowry Prohibition (Section 8A), and PCPNDT (Section 24).
5. **Landmark Precedents & Constitutional Ratios:** Authoritative Supreme Court interpretations establishing binding guidelines and striking down unconstitutional exceptions.

## 2. Nine-Act Thematic Architecture & Statutory Analysis

### Group A: Equality, Inclusion and Welfare

#### 1. Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 (RPwD Act)
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 49 of 2016; Enacted 27 Dec 2016; Commenced 19 April 2017. Repealed Persons with Disabilities Act, 1995.
- **Academic Structure:** 3 Chapters (`ch-soc-disab-01` to `ch-soc-disab-03`), 9 Topics, 50 Practice MCQs, 4 Historical PYQs.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 2(r):** Person with benchmark disability defined as someone with not less than 40% of a specified disability where defined in measurable terms.
  - **Section 2(zc) & Schedule:** 21 Specified Disabilities categorized into Physical (Locomotor, Leprosy Cured, Cerebral Palsy, Dwarfism, Muscular Dystrophy, Acid Attack Victims), Visual (Blindness, Low Vision), Hearing (Deaf, Hard of Hearing), Speech and Language, Intellectual (Specific Learning, Autism Spectrum), Mental Illness, Chronic Neurological (Multiple Sclerosis, Parkinson's), Blood Disorders (Haemophilia, Thalassemia, Sickle Cell), and Multiple Disabilities.
  - **Section 3:** Equality and non-discrimination; mandate of reasonable accommodation (does NOT require 40% benchmark disability).
  - **Section 12:** Access to justice; reasonable accommodation in courts, Braille/sign language interpreters.
  - **Section 32:** Reservation in higher education (minimum 5% seats).
  - **Section 34:** Reservation in public employment (minimum 4% total: 1% each for clauses a, b, c, and 1% combined for d & e).
  - **Sections 74 & 79:** Chief Commissioner (Union) & State Commissioner (States) with civil court powers under CPC.
  - **Sections 84 & 85:** Special Courts designated by State Government with concurrence of High Court Chief Justice; Special Public Prosecutor.
  - **Section 92:** Offence of atrocities against disabled persons (imprisonment 6 months to 5 years plus fine).
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *Vikas Kumar v. UPSC (2021) 5 SCC 370:* Benchmark disability (40%) is a condition for affirmative action/quotas, NOT a precondition for obtaining reasonable accommodation (scribe/extra time).
  - *Disabled Rights Group v. Union of India (2018) 2 SCC 397:* Mandatory 5% reservation in higher education under Section 32 strictly enforced across all law schools and universities.

#### 2. Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 56 of 2007; Enacted 29 Dec 2007; Commenced upon respective State Gazette notifications.
- **Academic Structure:** 3 Chapters (`ch-soc-parent-01` to `ch-soc-parent-03`), 9 Topics, 50 Practice MCQs, 1 Historical PYQ.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 2(b), (d), (h):** Child includes son, daughter, grandson, granddaughter (excludes minor); Parent includes biological, adoptive, or step-father/step-mother (whether senior citizen or not); Senior Citizen means citizen of India aged 60 years or above.
  - **Section 4:** Duty to maintain parents/senior citizens extending to children and childless senior citizens' relatives who are in possession of or would inherit their property.
  - **Section 7:** Maintenance Tribunal presided over by Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM).
  - **Section 9:** Maintenance order; monthly allowance capped at maximum ₹10,000 per month (as enacted; some state amendments remove cap).
  - **Section 15 & 16:** Appellate Tribunal presided over by District Magistrate (DM); right of appeal lies only to senior citizen/parent (as per text of Section 16(1), though High Courts read in reciprocal rights under Article 226).
  - **Section 17:** Absolute bar on legal representation before Maintenance Tribunal (parties cannot be represented by advocate without leave).
  - **Section 23:** Transfer of property by senior citizen to be void in certain circumstances (deemed made by fraud, coercion, or undue influence if transferee fails to provide basic amenities).
  - **Section 24:** Penalty for exposure and abandonment of senior citizen (imprisonment up to 3 months or fine up to ₹5,000 or both).
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *Sudesh Chhikara v. Ramti Devi (2022) SCC OnLine SC 1684:* For Section 23 to apply, the transfer document must contain an express condition (or implied circumstance) that transferee would provide basic amenities/physical needs.
  - *S. Vanitha v. Deputy Commissioner (2021) 15 SCC 730:* Harmonious construction between Senior Citizens Act 2007 and Domestic Violence Act 2005. Maintenance Tribunal cannot evict daughter-in-law summarily if she has shared household rights under PWDVA without balancing protections.

### Group B: Protection of Women

#### 3. Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 28 of 1961; Enacted 20 May 1961; Commenced 1 July 1961; Extensively amended in 1984 and 1986.
- **Academic Structure:** 3 Chapters (`ch-soc-dowry-01` to `ch-soc-dowry-03`), 9 Topics, 50 Practice MCQs, 5 Historical PYQs.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 2:** Definition of 'dowry' (property or valuable security given or agreed to be given directly or indirectly in connection with marriage). Excludes dower or mahr in the case of persons governed by Muslim Personal Law (Shariat).
  - **Section 3:** Penalty for giving or taking dowry: imprisonment not less than 5 years and fine not less than ₹15,000 or the amount of the value of such dowry, whichever is more. Exceptions: customary presents/gifts given at the time of marriage without any demand, entered in a verified list signed by both parties.
  - **Section 4:** Penalty for demanding dowry (direct or indirect): imprisonment not less than 6 months extending up to 2 years and fine up to ₹10,000.
  - **Section 4A:** Ban on advertisement offering share in property/money in consideration of marriage (imprisonment 6 months to 5 years or fine up to ₹15,000).
  - **Section 6:** Dowry to be for the benefit of the wife or her heirs (Stridhan trust). Must be transferred within 3 months of marriage/receipt/attaining age of 18; non-transfer punishable with imprisonment 6 months to 2 years.
  - **Section 7:** Cognizance by Judicial Magistrate First Class / Metropolitan Magistrate; upon police report, complaint by aggrieved person/parent, or recognized welfare institution.
  - **Section 8:** Offences to be cognizable for investigation, non-bailable, and non-compoundable.
  - **Section 8A:** Reverse burden of proof: where person is prosecuted for taking or abetting dowry, burden of proving he had not committed an offence is on him.
  - **Section 8B:** Dowry Prohibition Officers appointed by State Government with advisory boards.
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *Satvir Singh v. State of Punjab (2001) 8 SCC 633:* 'In connection with the marriage' in Section 2 is wider than 'in consideration of marriage' post-1984 amendment, covering demands made at or any time after marriage.
  - *Bobba Venkatesh v. State of A.P. (2024):* Distinction between customary gifts and dowry demand; lists under Section 3(2) rules must be strictly scrutinized.

#### 4. Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (PWDVA)
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 43 of 2005; Enacted 13 Sept 2005; Commenced 26 Oct 2006.
- **Academic Structure:** 3 Chapters (`ch-soc-dv-01` to `ch-soc-dv-03`), 9 Topics, 50 Practice MCQs, 2 Historical PYQs.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 2(a), (f), (q), (s):** Aggrieved person (any woman in domestic relationship); Domestic relationship (living together in shared household through consanguinity, marriage, relationship in nature of marriage, adoption, or joint family); Respondent (any adult male, or female relative of husband/male partner per *Hiral P. Harsora*); Shared household (household where aggrieved person lives or lived in a domestic relationship).
  - **Section 3:** Definition of Domestic Violence: physical, sexual, verbal, emotional, and economic abuse, including dowry demands.
  - **Section 12:** Application to Magistrate (Judicial Magistrate First Class / Metropolitan Magistrate); first hearing within 3 days; disposal target 60 days.
  - **Section 17:** Right to reside in a shared household; aggrieved woman cannot be evicted except in accordance with procedure established by law.
  - **Section 18:** Protection orders (prohibiting acts of violence, communication, entering workplace).
  - **Section 19:** Residence orders (restraining respondent from dispossessing, directing alternative accommodation, or removing respondent from shared household).
  - **Section 20:** Monetary relief (medical expenses, loss of earnings, maintenance).
  - **Section 21:** Temporary custody orders for children.
  - **Section 22:** Compensation orders for mental torture and emotional distress.
  - **Section 23:** Power of Magistrate to grant interim and ex-parte orders on affidavit (Form III).
  - **Section 31:** Penalty for breach of protection order by respondent (cognizable and non-bailable, imprisonment up to 1 year or fine up to ₹20,000 or both).
  - **Section 32:** Cognizance and proof; upon sole testimony of the aggrieved person, court may conclude breach occurred.
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *Hiral P. Harsora v. Kusum Narottamdas Harsora (2016) 10 SCC 165:* Struck down the words 'adult male' in Section 2(q) as violative of Article 14. An application under PWDVA can now be filed against female respondents (mothers-in-law, sisters-in-law) who perpetrate violence.
  - *Satish Chander Ahuja v. Sneha Ahuja (2021) 1 SCC 414:* Overruled *S.R. Batra v. Taruna Batra (2007)*. A shared household under Section 2(s) does not require that property belongs exclusively to the husband; if the wife lived with the husband in a domestic relationship in property belonging to in-laws, it constitutes a shared household.
  - *Rajnesh v. Neha (2021) 2 SCC 324:* Mandatory Affidavits of Assets and Liabilities in all maintenance applications across CrPC 125, HMA 24, and DV Act Section 20. Overlapping maintenance orders to be adjusted, not multiplied.

#### 5. Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (POSH) Act, 2013
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 14 of 2013; Enacted 22 April 2013; Commenced 9 Dec 2013. Statutory codification of *Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997)*.
- **Academic Structure:** 3 Chapters (`ch-soc-posh-01` to `ch-soc-posh-03`), 9 Topics, 50 Practice MCQs, 1 Historical PYQ.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 2(a), (o):** Aggrieved woman (any woman of any age, employed or not, alleging sexual harassment); Workplace (any department, organization, undertaking, private sector, hospital, sports institute, dwelling place or house, and any place visited by employee arising out of employment including transportation).
  - **Section 3:** Prevention of sexual harassment; hostile work environment, preferential or detrimental treatment connected to sexual advances.
  - **Section 4:** Internal Committee (IC): Mandatory for every workplace with 10 or more employees. Composition: Presiding Officer (senior woman employee), at least 2 members committed to women's cause, 1 external member (NGO/association), with at least 50% women representation. Tenure maximum 3 years.
  - **Section 6 & 7:** Local Committee (LC): Constituted by District Officer in every district to receive complaints from establishments with less than 10 workers or where complaint is against the employer himself.
  - **Section 9:** Complaint within 3 months of incident (extendable by another 3 months for sufficient cause).
  - **Section 10:** Conciliation before inquiry; strictly at the request of the aggrieved woman; NO monetary settlement permitted.
  - **Section 11:** Inquiry into complaint; IC/LC has powers of a Civil Court under CPC (summoning, discovery, oaths); inquiry must be completed within 90 days.
  - **Section 12:** Interim relief during inquiry (transfer, leave up to 3 months in addition to entitled leave).
  - **Section 13:** Inquiry report within 10 days of completion; employer must act on recommendations within 60 days.
  - **Section 14:** Action for malicious/false complaint (requires clear malicious intent; mere inability to substantiate is NOT malicious).
  - **Section 16:** Absolute prohibition of publication or making known contents of complaint, inquiry proceedings, and identities of parties (RTI Act 2005 overridden).
  - **Section 26:** Penalties for non-compliance by employer (fine up to ₹50,000; repeat offence doubles fine and can lead to cancellation of business licence).
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan (1997) 7 SCC 323:* Historic foundation deriving binding workplace guidelines from Articles 14, 19(1)(g), 21 and CEDAW.
  - *Aureliano Fernandes v. State of Goa (2023) SCC OnLine SC 621:* Supreme Court issued nationwide directions highlighting serious lapses in IC formations and mandated rigorous compliance audits across all public and private institutions.

#### 6. Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act, 1986 (IRWA)
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 60 of 1986; Enacted 23 Dec 1986; Commenced 2 Oct 1987.
- **Academic Structure:** 3 Chapters (`ch-soc-irwa-01` to `ch-soc-irwa-03`), 9 Topics, 45 Practice MCQs, 3 Historical PYQs.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 2(a), (c):** Advertisement includes notice, circular, label, wrapper, or other document, and visible representation by light, sound, smoke, or gas; Indecent representation of women means the depiction in any manner of the figure of a woman, her form or body or any part thereof in such a way as to have the effect of being indecent, or derogatory to, or denigrating women, or is likely to deprave, corrupt or injure the public morality or morals.
  - **Section 3:** Prohibition of advertisements containing indecent representation of women (strict publication/production ban).
  - **Section 4:** Prohibition of publication, distribution, circulation, sale, hire of books, pamphlets, paper, slide, film, writing, drawing, painting, photograph, or figure containing indecent representation of women.
  - **Section 4 Provisos (Statutory Exceptions):**
    1. Publication justified as being for the public good in the interest of science, literature, art, or learning.
    2. Ancient monuments or archaeological sites under Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958.
    3. Any temple or idol used for religious purposes.
    4. Films certified under Part II of the Cinematograph Act, 1952.
  - **Section 5:** Powers of search and seizure: any Gazetted Officer authorized by State Government may enter and search any place, seize offending materials, with mandatory search warrant protections under CrPC/BNSS.
  - **Section 6:** Penalties:
    - First conviction: Imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to ₹2,000.
    - Second or subsequent conviction: Imprisonment not less than 6 months extending up to 5 years and fine not less than ₹10,000 extending up to ₹1,00,000.
  - **Section 7 & 8:** Offences by companies; offences to be bailable and cognizable.
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *Aveek Sarkar v. State of West Bengal (2014) 4 SCC 257:* Formally discarded the antiquated Hicklin Test (*Regina v. Hicklin, 1868*) in India and adopted the 'Contemporary Community Standards Test'. A photograph of Boris Becker and his fiancée was held not to deprave morals or constitute indecent representation when viewed in its proper artistic and sports context.

### Group C: Reproductive Health and Child Protection

#### 7. Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques Act, 1994 (PCPNDT Act)
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 54 of 1994; Enacted 20 Sept 1994; Commenced 1 Jan 1996; Extensively amended in 2003 (bringing 'pre-conception' sex selection into scope).
- **Academic Structure:** 3 Chapters (`ch-soc-pcpndt-01` to `ch-soc-pcpndt-03`), 9 Topics, 50 Practice MCQs, 2 Historical PYQs.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 2(o), (p):** Sex selection defined as any procedure, technique, test or administration of medicine for the purpose of ensuring or increasing the probability that an embryo will be of a particular sex; Genetic Clinic/Laboratory/Counseling Centre.
  - **Section 3A & 3B:** Absolute prohibition of sex selection before or after conception; prohibition on sale of ultrasound machines to unregistered bodies.
  - **Section 4:** Regulation of pre-natal diagnostic techniques; permitted ONLY for detecting chromosomal abnormalities, genetic metabolic diseases, haemoglobinopathies, sex-linked genetic diseases, or congenital anomalies.
  - **Section 5:** Written consent of pregnant woman (Form G) and prohibition on communicating sex of foetus in any manner to anyone.
  - **Section 6:** Absolute prohibition on determination of sex by Genetic Clinic, laboratory, or medical practitioner.
  - **Section 17:** Appropriate Authority (multi-member body at State/District/Sub-district level including Medical Officer, woman representative, and law officer).
  - **Section 17A:** Powers of Appropriate Authority: civil court powers (summoning, discovery, production of records, issuing search warrants).
  - **Section 22:** Prohibition of advertisement relating to sex selection (imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to ₹10,000).
  - **Section 23:** Penalties:
    - Medical practitioner / Genetic centre owner: First offence: imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to ₹10,000; Second/subsequent: imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to ₹50,000. Reporting to State Medical Council for suspension (first offence) and removal of name (subsequent).
    - Pregnant woman: Presumed to have been compelled by husband/in-laws unless proved otherwise (Section 24); husband/relatives punishable under Section 23.
  - **Section 27:** All offences are cognizable, non-bailable, and non-compoundable.
  - **Section 28:** Cognizance ONLY upon complaint by the Appropriate Authority or an officer authorized by it, or by a person who has given 15 days notice to the Authority. Police CANNOT register FIR directly under PCPNDT Act without Appropriate Authority's complaint.
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *Voluntary Health Association of Punjab v. Union of India (2013) 4 SCC 1:* Supreme Court ordered nationwide strict implementation, seizure of unregistered ultrasound machines, and mandatory maintenance of Form F registers.
  - *Federation of Obstetrics and Gynaecological Societies of India (FOGSI) v. Union of India (2019) 6 SCC 426:* Upheld constitutional validity of Section 23(1) and (2); held that non-maintenance of clerical records / Form F is inextricably linked to the offence of sex determination and cannot be diluted.

#### 8. Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971 (as amended 2021)
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 34 of 1971; Enacted 10 Aug 1971; Commenced 1 April 1972; Major amendment via Medical Termination of Pregnancy (Amendment) Act, 2021 (Act 8 of 2021, commenced 24 Sept 2021).
- **Academic Structure:** 3 Chapters (`ch-soc-mtp-01` to `ch-soc-mtp-03`), 9 Topics, 50 Practice MCQs, 3 Historical PYQs.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 3(2)(a):** Gestation up to 20 weeks: Termination permitted on opinion of ONE Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP).
  - **Section 3(2)(b):** Gestation from 20 to 24 weeks: Termination permitted on opinion of TWO Registered Medical Practitioners for specified categories of vulnerable women (survivors of sexual assault/rape/incest, minors, change of marital status during pregnancy, women with physical disabilities, mentally ill women, foetal malformations, emergency humanitarian situations per MTP Rules 2021).
  - **Section 3(2B):** Gestation beyond 24 weeks: Gestational limit DOES NOT apply where termination is necessitated by substantial foetal abnormalities diagnosed by a State-level Medical Board.
  - **Section 3(2C):** Explanation 1: Contraceptive failure expanded to cover 'any woman or her partner' (replacing the archaic 'married woman or her husband').
  - **Section 3(4):** Consent requirements:
    - Pregnant woman who has attained 18 years of age: Her own consent ALONE is required. Husband's or family's consent is NOT legally required.
    - Minor (under 18 years) or mentally ill woman: Written consent of her guardian is mandatory.
  - **Section 4:** Places where pregnancy may be terminated: hospital established/maintained by Government, or approved private place.
  - **Section 5(1):** Emergency exception: Requirements of gestation limits and opinion of RMPs do not apply if termination is immediately necessary to save the life of the pregnant woman.
  - **Section 5A:** Protection of privacy of woman: No RMP or medical personnel shall disclose name and particulars of woman except to person authorized by law (penalty up to 1 year imprisonment or fine or both).
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *X v. Principal Secretary, Health and Family Welfare Department, GNCTD (2023) 9 SCC 433:* Historic Constitution-affirming judgment holding that unmarried and single women have equal right to terminate pregnancy between 20-24 weeks under Rule 3B. Bodily autonomy and reproductive freedom are intrinsic components of Article 21. Also held that marital rape constitutes sexual assault for the purposes of MTP Act Rule 3B.
  - *Suchita Srivastava v. Chandigarh Administration (2009) 9 SCC 1:* Woman's reproductive choice is an inseparable facet of personal liberty and privacy under Article 21. Mentally retarded woman cannot be forced to abort if she expresses consent to carry pregnancy.

#### 9. Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 (POCSO Act)
- **Act No. & Date:** Act 32 of 2012; Enacted 19 June 2012; Commenced 14 Nov 2012; Amending Act 25 of 2019 (adding death penalty for aggravated penetrative sexual assault).
- **Academic Structure:** 4 Chapters (`ch-soc-pocso-01` to `ch-soc-pocso-04`), 12 Topics, 60 Practice MCQs, 2 Historical PYQs.
- **Key Statutory Provisions:**
  - **Section 2(1)(d):** Child defined gender-neutrally as any person below the age of 18 years.
  - **Sections 3 & 4:** Penetrative Sexual Assault (minimum imprisonment 10 years extending to life imprisonment and fine).
  - **Sections 5 & 6:** Aggravated Penetrative Sexual Assault (by police officer, public servant, armed forces, management/staff of hospital/educational institution, gang assault, on child below 12 years, causing grievous hurt/death). Punishment: minimum 20 years extending to imprisonment for remainder of natural life or DEATH (post-2019 amendment) and fine.
  - **Sections 7 & 8:** Sexual Assault (punishment 3 to 5 years plus fine).
  - **Sections 9 & 10:** Aggravated Sexual Assault (punishment 5 to 7 years plus fine).
  - **Sections 11 & 12:** Sexual Harassment (punishment up to 3 years plus fine).
  - **Sections 13, 14 & 15:** Use of child for pornographic purposes, storage of child sexual abuse material (CSAM).
  - **Section 19:** Mandatory reporting of offences: Any person (including medical personnel) having knowledge or apprehension of an offence must report to Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU) or local police. Failure to report punishable under Section 21 (up to 6 months imprisonment or fine or both).
  - **Section 23:** Absolute ban on disclosing the identity of the child in media or public domain (punishment 6 months to 1 year).
  - **Section 24:** Child-friendly recording of statement (at residence of child, in plain clothes, no confrontation with accused).
  - **Section 28:** Designated Special Courts (Sessions Court designated by State Government with concurrence of High Court Chief Justice).
  - **Sections 29 & 30:** Statutory Presumption of Guilt and Culpable Mental State: Once prosecution proves the baseline act, the Special Court SHALL presume the accused committed the offence and had the requisite culpable mental state, unless the contrary is proved by the accused.
  - **Section 33:** Special Court procedure: Child not to be detained overnight in police station; in-camera trial; questions put through presiding judge; frequent breaks.
  - **Section 35:** Period for recording evidence (within 30 days of taking cognizance) and completion of trial (within 1 year of taking cognizance).
  - **Section 42A:** Overriding effect: POCSO provisions prevail over any other inconsistent law for the time being in force.
- **Landmark Precedents:**
  - *Attorney General for India v. Satish (2022) 5 SCC 545:* Supreme Court reversed the Bombay High Court's 'skin-to-skin contact' judgment. Held that physical contact with sexual intent without skin-to-skin touch constitutes sexual assault under Section 7 of POCSO Act.
  - *Independent Thought v. Union of India (2017) 10 SCC 800:* Struck down Exception 2 to Section 375 of IPC as unconstitutional to the extent it protected marital sexual intercourse with a minor wife between 15-18 years, harmonizing criminal law with POCSO Act.
  - *Alakh Alok Srivastava v. Union of India (2018) 11 SCC 284:* Directives for speedy investigation (2 months) and trial (6 months) in child sexual assault cases, and establishment of dedicated POCSO Fast Track Special Courts (FTSCs).
